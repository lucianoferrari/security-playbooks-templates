# CISO First 90 Days Plan

A structured plan for a new security leader. The goal of the first 90 days is **not** to fix everything — it's to understand the business, earn trust, stop the bleeding, and leave with a funded, prioritized roadmap.

---

## Days 1–30: Listen and assess

### Business understanding
- [ ] Meet every executive peer: what keeps them up at night, how security has helped or hurt them
- [ ] Understand revenue drivers, key customers, regulatory obligations, and upcoming initiatives (M&A, new markets, AI adoption)
- [ ] Read the last two board decks, audit findings, pen-test reports, and any regulator correspondence

### Program inventory
- [ ] Team: roles, skills, morale, attrition risk, open reqs
- [ ] Budget: run vs. change, contract renewals in the next 12 months
- [ ] Tooling: what's deployed, what's actually used, what's shelfware
- [ ] Policies: exist? current? enforced?
- [ ] Compliance calendar: PCI, SOC 2, ISO 27001, SOX, privacy, state/sector regulators

### Risk snapshot
- [ ] Crown-jewel data and systems identified
- [ ] External attack surface review
- [ ] Identity posture: MFA coverage, privileged-access controls, joiner/mover/leaver
- [ ] Backup and recovery tested? When?
- [ ] Detection coverage mapped to the top 10 realistic attack scenarios
- [ ] Third-party and AI/LLM risk exposure

### Quick wins to identify (do not announce yet)
- Low-effort, high-visibility fixes that build credibility

**Deliverable (Day 30):** One-page "what I found" brief to the CEO/CIO — honest, non-alarmist, no asks yet.

---

## Days 31–60: Prioritize and stabilize

- [ ] Execute 3–5 quick wins (e.g., MFA gaps closed, legacy auth blocked, critical patch backlog burned down, incident-response contacts verified)
- [ ] Run a **tabletop exercise** with executives — nothing aligns a leadership team faster
- [ ] Establish the **risk register** with business owners, not just security owners
- [ ] Define the security operating model: what's centralized, what's embedded, decision rights
- [ ] Choose the framework you'll measure against (NIST CSF 2.0 / CIS Controls / ISO 27001) and baseline current maturity
- [ ] Set up a metrics cadence: a small set of outcome metrics, not activity counts
- [ ] Clarify the AI governance stance: inventory, acceptable use, review gate for new AI systems

**Deliverable (Day 60):** Prioritized risk register and draft roadmap reviewed with peers.

---

## Days 61–90: Commit and communicate

- [ ] Finalize the **12–18 month roadmap** with three horizons: stabilize, mature, differentiate
- [ ] Build the **business case**: investment tied to risk reduction, compliance obligations, and revenue enablement (customer trust, faster deals)
- [ ] Present to the executive team and board/audit committee — see the [board report template](board-security-report-template.md)
- [ ] Reorganize or re-level the team if needed; open the critical reqs
- [ ] Publish the security operating model and key policies
- [ ] Establish partnerships: Legal, Privacy, Internal Audit, Engineering, Product, Finance

**Deliverable (Day 90):** Board-approved strategy and funded roadmap.

---

## Principles

1. **Business first.** Every control maps to a business risk or obligation someone outside security recognizes.
2. **Credibility before capital.** Deliver quick wins before asking for budget.
3. **Fewer, better metrics.** Trend lines over snapshots.
4. **Assume compromise.** Recovery capability is the control that matters most on your worst day.
5. **Write things down.** Decisions, rationale, and risk acceptances — your future self and your auditors will thank you.
